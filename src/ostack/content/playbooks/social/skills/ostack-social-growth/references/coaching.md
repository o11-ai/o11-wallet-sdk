# Coaching the person's social growth

Point out what is holding their growth back while you work and in every report, without being asked. How to say it, and what you may change yourself versus what needs their yes, is in the ostack-setup skill's `references/first-run.md` ("Coach without being asked"). Comments follow [comments.md](comments.md); captions follow the ostack-content-calendar skill's `references/captions.md`.

## Red flags

Name at most three, worst first, each with an example from their own account, why it costs growth and the fix.

1. **A niche too wide.** Search terms that pull in everyone (a whole industry, a buzzword), so follows and comments land on people who will never care.
2. **Comments that could go anywhere.** "Great post!", praise with nothing specific, or a pitch. They get ignored or muted; see comments.md.
3. **A profile that does not convert.** The bio does not say who they help and how, no link, or a pinned post that is old or off-topic. New visitors leave without following.
4. **Engaging but not posting.** Following and commenting with no posts of their own in weeks gives people nothing to follow back for.
5. **Captions that bury the point.** The hook is not in the first line, the call to action is missing, or the same caption is pasted on every platform.
6. **The wrong accounts.** Most engagement goes to large accounts whose replies are flooded, or to brands rather than people.

An account with none of these: say so in one line.

## Their posts versus yours

- Comments and captions you draft: fix the flags before they go out, and say so in one line.
- Their own bio, pinned post or captions: show a better version next to theirs and ask for a one-word yes. Never edit their profile yourself.

## After results come in

Every report ends with a short "What's working" note from the run's own numbers: follow-backs, replies to comments and new followers per platform and per search term. Name the search terms and kinds of posts that brought follow-backs and suggest dropping the ones that brought none after a week. Say plainly when there is too little data yet.
