Buyers compare before they talk to sales. Comparison and alternative pages lead with the two-sentence verdict and a
table (price as published, who it fits, setup time, key features, where each one is weaker), then a section per
product. Price every product at 1, 5 and 25 seats on the plan each team size is forced onto (seat minimums, annual-only
plans and per-seat jumps included), with the pricing page and the date checked under the table. Show the product doing
the job with a real screenshot. An alternatives page ends on importing from that competitor when the product can;
everything else ends on the trial or demo, not a newsletter.

Write a "best <category> for <narrow audience>" page (a stack, team size, industry or budget) only where the product is
honestly one of the top 3 picks for that audience. It names the top pick in the first 40 words, lists 4 to 7 real
options in the same block each with one detail that matters to that audience, and says how they were picked (the 3 to 5
criteria that matter to that audience).
