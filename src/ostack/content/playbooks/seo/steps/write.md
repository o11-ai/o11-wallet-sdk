Read the team's `voice.md` and `seo/write.md`, the top 5 results for the search (`o11 call o11.web.extract` on each),
and what AI assistants answer for it. Then write the page that is plainly better for the person searching: more
specific, more current and easier to act on. Never rewrite a competitor's page in other words.

Every piece:
- **Answers first.** The first two sentences answer the search directly; AI assistants and featured snippets quote them.
- **Covers what the top results cover**, then adds what they miss: real numbers, screenshots, steps from using the
  product, an opinion the team holds. Facts come from sources you read, linked inline. Never invent customers, results,
  quotes or statistics.
- **Comparisons and alternatives** name each product's real strengths, prices as published (with the date checked) and
  who each one fits; the team's product is not best at everything.
- **Is easy to scan:** short paragraphs, descriptive headings that match the questions people ask, tables for comparisons,
  numbered steps for how-tos, one idea per section.
- **Links:** 3 to 6 links to the team's related pages and the product page the piece supports, with descriptive anchor
  text, and links out to the sources it cites.
- **Has the parts search needs:** title under 60 characters with the search first, description under 160, a slug from
  the search, Article structured data with author and dates, an FAQ only from real questions people ask, and an image
  with alt text when it helps.
- **Ends on the action** in `seo/site.md`.

Length follows the search: as long as the top results need, rarely under 1,000 or over 3,000 words. Read it back once as
the person searching; cut anything they would skip.
