Links come from being worth citing, never from buying them, swapping them or trading in schemes. Find the sites that
already link to two or more competitors and not to the team (backlink gaps), the pages AI assistants cite for the
team's buyer questions (the latest tracking report gives each an action), directories and review sites buyers in this
market actually use, partners and integrations that list their partners, and posts that mention the team without
linking. Directories and review sites are listed for the team to submit; never submit to them yourself.

**Clean links only.** Read each competitor's backlinks with a full list (`research` names the tools) and keep a link
only when it is followed (dofollow), sits in the body of the page rather than a footer, sidebar or author box, counts
once per linking domain, and the linking page itself gets more than 10 search visits a month. Drop linking pages with
150 or more outbound links (sites that sell posts), and tag, author, archive and comment pages. When a tool does not
return one of these fields, say which filter could not be applied. A competitor whose clean list is short is beatable:
say so with the number.

**Fake authority.** A site with a domain rating (or the tool's authority score) of 40 or more that ranks for fewer than
100 keywords got its authority from links that do not help it rank. Flag it, never copy its links, and when it is the
team's own site, say that its links are not the problem to solve.

**The exact place.** For each site worth asking, pick one page of theirs that ranks (it gets search visits or ranks
top 20 for a search close to the team's product), never a home, login, pricing, careers or legal page. Name the heading
or paragraph where a mention fits and write the one sentence it would add, true and useful to their reader. When no
sentence fits naturally, there is no link to ask for: drop it.

**Badge loops.** Check whether the product earns links as people use it: a "Made with <brand>" badge on what customers
publish (pages, forms, reports, status pages), public share links that can be indexed, or embeds that credit the
team. Suggest the ones that fit as product changes, linking to the home page with the brand name as the link text,
never keywords. Never add hidden links.

**Hand-off.** List each with the page, the paragraph, why it would link, and who to ask (from the page's byline or the
site's team page; mark a guessed address unverified). When the gtm playbook is set up, hand the list to
ostack-outbound as an audience (a short `gtm/audiences/links/` with the pitch); otherwise put it in the report. Rules
for every pitch:
- It names their ranking page and the exact spot, and asks for the link before saying what is in it for them.
- Emails in one batch vary in subject, opening, ask and length; no two in a row read alike.
- What they get is a better page for their reader, a case study, a partner listing or credit; never money and never a
  link swap.

Never send outreach from this playbook.
