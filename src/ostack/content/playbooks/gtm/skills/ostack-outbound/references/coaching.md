# Coaching the user's outreach

The user wants replies, not just sends. Act like a sharp friend who has run a lot of outbound: say plainly what will hurt their reply rate, fix it for them, and keep it short. Coaching never adds a round of questions: it rides inside a message you were sending anyway. [writing.md](writing.md) is the standard every point here comes from.

## Red flags in a template or message

Check for these, worst first. Name at most three, one line each, in plain words, with why it costs replies.

1. **Opens about the sender.** "We're…", "I'm reaching out…", "My name is…". The reader decides in the first line whether this is about them.
2. **Could go to anyone.** Only the name and company change. It fails the one-person bar in writing.md, and the outbound run will defer leads it cannot make specific.
3. **Too long.** Over about 90 words, a feature list, or more than three short paragraphs.
4. **A big or double ask.** "30 minutes this week?", a calendar link plus a question, or two asks at once. One small, easy yes works best.
5. **Sounds like AI or sales.** The openers, words and punctuation listed in writing.md, or flattery with nothing specific behind it.
6. **Nothing believable.** No proof (a customer, a number, a result), or a claim `company.md` does not back.
7. **Hurts delivery.** Links, tracking, attachments or images in a first message; a fake `re:` or `fwd:`; a clickbait subject.
8. **The ask does not fit the price.** A 30-minute sales call for a cheap self-serve product costs the reader more than it is worth to them; ask them to try it, or a yes or no question, instead. A call fits when the deal is big enough for one.
9. **Pushy or guilt-trip lines.** "Just bumping this", "I'll assume you're not interested", fake deadlines.

A template with none of these: say it looks good in one line and use it as is. Never invent problems to sound useful.

## When the user gave you a template

They pasted it in chat or named a file: it is theirs, so ask before replacing it.

- In the same reply as the preview, say kindly what will cost replies (the red flags above, at most three), then show your rewrite under it. Keep their offer, ask and personality; change only what fixes the flags.
- Draft the preview messages with your version and say so, then end with one choice for them: keep yours, use mine, or test both.
- Save theirs as `gtm/template-a.md` right away (in the audience's folder for another audience); it is theirs. **Test both** adds the rewrite as `gtm/template-b.md`, so new people split between them and the weekly note compares replies. **Keep yours** changes nothing. **Use mine** replaces theirs with the rewrite.
- If they keep theirs, use it exactly and do not raise the same points again unless the numbers later show it is losing.

## When you found a template yourself

From their sent mail, a CRM sequence or a doc in their repo: they did not ask you to use it. If it has red flags, draft the first emails with an improved version automatically and say so in one line ("I tightened the template from your sent mail: shorter, opens on them, one small ask. Say 'use my original' to switch back."). If it has none, use it as is.

## When there is no template

Write in the default shape (the write step), matched to how the people they reach write. Do not ask whether they want a template.

## Saying it well

- Kind and direct: "This will likely get few replies because it opens about Ledgerly, not them." Never "this is bad", never a lecture, never a list of rules.
- Concrete: point at the exact line and show the fix.
- Honest about size: a small issue is "minor"; do not oversell a rewrite.
- Short: the critique is at most three lines plus the rewrite. The preview drafts are the real argument.

## After results come in

On the first outbound run of each week (Monday in the team's timezone, or the first run after), or whenever the user asks how outreach is doing, read the record tool for the last 28 days and count, per `o11_template` and audience: first messages sent, people who replied (not counting opt-outs and bounces), and meetings booked. Add one short "How your messages are doing" note to the report:

- Under 20 sends for a template: say it is too early to judge, with the counts so far.
- Two templates, each with 20 or more sends and one replying at least twice as often: name the winner and suggest pausing the other (in a scheduled run, suggest only; when the user is present, offer to do it).
- Reply rate under 2% after 40 or more sends: name the most likely cause from the red flags above or from who is being reached (qualify), and offer one specific change with an A/B test against the current template.
- Otherwise: one line with the numbers and nothing to change.

Never change a template, pause one or rewrite files in a scheduled run. Suggest, and act only when the user agrees.
