# Writing outreach people reply to

Inboxes are full of AI outbound that all sounds the same. A message only works if it reads like a real person spent real time on this one recipient. Read this before the research and write steps, and again before follow-ups. The user's own files (voice, template, write) still win.

## The bar
- **One person only.** No message could go to anyone else and still work. If it could, research more or defer the lead with the reason.
- **Visible effort.** The reader should be able to tell someone looked hard: something only a person who dug in would know or notice.
- **The reply test.** Read it as the recipient, on a busy day. Would you reply? Assume no, and say plainly why yes before you send. When founders are asked this about their own drafts, the honest answer is usually no.
- **Fewer and better.** A smaller batch of strong messages beats a full daily limit of generic ones. Deferring a lead with no strong angle is the right call, not a failure.

## Earning the reply
Pick the strongest one or two that are true for this person:
- **Try their product.** Use the public pages, docs, demo video or free tool, and say one specific thing you saw (a step that confused you, a feature that stood out, how it compares to what they claim). Signing up, or typing anything into their app or forms, needs the user's say-so in their files (ask once when present), and never uses fake or anyone else's details.
- **A screenshot** of something specific to them: their page with one point marked, a bug, their listing, a public metric, a quick mockup of the idea applied to their product. Use it only when it carries the point and the send tool can include an image, and keep it real and unedited apart from the marking.
- **Something personal and well researched:** a line from their talk, podcast or post, a decision they made publicly, a hire, a customer they won, a problem their own customers describe in public reviews. Quote or link it exactly.
- **Their situation, in their words:** connect the fact to a task or decision they own right now, then the offer in one line.

Never fake effort. No claim of having tried, read or watched something you did not, and no screenshot of anything you did not see.

## Sound like them, and like the founder
- **Speak the ICP's language.** Read how these people write (their posts, site copy, forum comments, changelogs, job posts) and use their words, register and level of detail. Mirror their jargon only where they use it.
- **Tech founders and startups:** short, casual, often all lowercase, like a message to a peer. Confirm the style once with the user and save it in `voice.md`.
- **Write as the sender.** Use `voice.md`: their samples, phrasing, punctuation, sign-off and quirks. If there are no samples, ask for two to five real messages they sent that got replies. Never write in a "sales" register they would not use themselves.

## What gives away AI outbound
Remove all of these:
- Openers such as "I hope this finds you well", "I came across", "I noticed", "Quick question", "Love what you're building", "In today's fast-paced world".
- Words such as leverage, streamline, unlock, empower, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust, synergy, elevate.
- Em dashes, lists of three, rhetorical questions, bullet points, bold text, and perfectly even paragraphs.
- Flattery with nothing specific behind it, and fake familiarity ("as a fellow founder" when the sender is not one).
- A sign-off or title the sender never uses.

## Shape
- **Subject:** two to five words, specific to them, the way a colleague would title it (lowercase when that is the style). No clickbait, no fake `re:` or `fwd:`.
- **Body:** under about 90 words. Their fact first, then why it matters to them, then the offer in one line.
- **One ask,** easy to say yes to: a short call, a yes or no question, or "want me to send the screenshot of the rest?".
- **No tracking links or attachments** in a first message. One inline screenshot is fine when it makes the point.

## Templates
A user's template keeps its fixed words. The parts it leaves open carry the effort above; a template filled with name and company only fails the one-person bar, so defer instead.

## Follow-ups
Each follow-up adds one new thing found since (a fresh fact, a screenshot, a sharper question). Same voice, shorter each time, never "just bumping this".
